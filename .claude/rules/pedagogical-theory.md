The design is grounded in five learning theories. Understanding these is essential when making UX and content decisions.

**Constructivism (Piaget, Vygotsky)** — Learners build knowledge by doing. Every module has a hands-on cryptographic lab (PR8) rather than just reading content. Labs are scenario-driven to connect abstract concepts to recognisable situations.

**Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)** — Three needs: autonomy, competence, relatedness. Autonomy → flexible paths, optional challenges. Competence → explicit learning objectives, immediate corrective feedback (PR4), mastery progression. Relatedness → opt-in cohort leaderboard (suppressed below 10 members to protect privacy).

**Flow Theory (Csikszentmihalyi)** — Challenge must match skill. Drives the 4-level difficulty taxonomy (PR2) and the adaptive difficulty system (Phase 4). Tiered questions (core/consol/boost) maintain the flow channel for diverse skill levels.

**FRAME Model (Koole)** — Mobile-first, not desktop-ported. The interface is designed for 720p touch interaction from the ground up, not adapted from a desktop layout.

**Universal Design for Learning (CAST)** — Multiple representations (text + concept-boxes + code examples), multiple modes of expression (quizzes + labs), multiple engagement strategies (gamification + spaced repetition + daily challenges).
